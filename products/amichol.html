Vital reactions such as protein synthesis, DNA and RNA synthesis, fat metabolism etc., are critically dependent upon the availabilityof labile methyl groups.

The body's requirement for methylgroup is primarily met by methionine which is willing methyl donor. However, methionine is required for several other functions in the body. Hence, methyl groups are obtained from other donors like choline and betane and are used up to convert methionine back in to its original state. Today's least cost diets are often marginally deficient in nutrients, especially like methionine, lysine, choline etc.,Supplementation of such nutrients has become an essential part of formulating poultry diets. Since methionine is acostly nutrient, restoration of methionine activity by use of choline chloride, an inexpensive alternative, is in common practice.

But,the actual requirement, the level of usage and the degree of availability varies from bird to bird, farm to farm and feed to feed. Choline, basically is not a methyl donor by itself. It has to be converted in to a liable donor, betaine, before itcan donate methyl group. This conversion is found to be very efficient in animals. The uptake and conversion of choline is also adversly affected by possible interaction with other feed components, gut infection, infection, malabsorption, liver problems, low intramitochondria organic calcium/inorganic phosphorous, deficiency/ inability of mitochondrial phospholipase, presence of ionophores/EDTA in diet etc.

The availability of the choline to the birds, thus is always incomplete. The solution to this problem is not give more choline chloride. Rather, Giving the optimum quantity of choline chloride and improving its availability would be the better option.
