NRC (1994) recommends a requirement of Vitamin K ranging between 0.4 to 1.75 mg/kg of diet for poultry. Subclinical and Clinical Coccidiosis.

Different Stages of coccidia in chickens apper both within the host as well as outside. The developmental stages in the chicken give rise toa microscopic egg (called an oocyst) that is passed out in the droppings. Under proper conditions oftemperature and moisture, the oocyst develops within two days, toform a sporulated oocyst, which is capable ofinfectingotherchickens.Atthis stage, the oocystcontains eight bodies (called sporozoites),each of which is capable of entering a cell in the chicken's intestine after the oocyst is eaten. when sporozoites enter the cells, theydivide manytimes producing eithera fewor manyoffspring (merozoites).

The numbers produced depend on the species of coccidia involved. Each merozoite,in turn, may enter another intestinal cell. This cycle may be repeated several times, because ofthis cyclic multiplication, large numbers of intestinal cells are destroyed causing hemorrhages. Oocysts remain viable in litterfor months. In thisway, they can contaminate a farm from year to year. Oocysts are killed by freezing,extreme dryness, high temperatures and by use of effectivedrugs. Vitamin K1 is not utilized bythe feed industry, due tocost and lack ofa stabilized form. Instead, water-souble (Vitamin K3) salts are used to provide Vitamin Kactivity in feeds.

Because of poorstability, menadione is not used as the pure vitamin,but is produced as water-soluble salts. Water-soluble derivatives of menadione, including menadione sodium bisulfite (MSB), are the principal forms of Vitamin K included in commercial diets. The menadioneconcentration is 50% for MSB,45.4% for MPB, and 33% for MSBC.
